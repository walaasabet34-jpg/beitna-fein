## Canon

Hoda stays with Fatma after her relative Sonia is admitted to hospital. Naima knows Sonia; she helps find the mother's current contact through her previous workplace. No fictional government tracing procedure is asserted. A possible reply is checked using age and workplace, not a grandfather's name alone. The genuine mother's reply arrives on 18 November: Hoda recognises a personal detail. The mother plans to visit at the beginning of December, before the danger of the later illness is known.

Initial illness occurs from 18 November; clinical follow-up and isolation are described without treatment instructions. Severe deterioration, urgent contact and hospital care occur on 30 November. Hoda dies that night despite treatment. The mother arrives after Fatma returns; Naima tells her before any player-controlled meeting. The final chapter runs 1–4 December. These are fictional family events, not a typical clinical course or a claim about postal/travel times in 1973.

## Writing

- Fatma speaks practically. Give the player things to say or do, not labels such as “act honestly”.
- Hoda asks, counts, gets bored, makes mistakes and helps. Humour comes from her priorities, not a punchline in every response.
- Naima conveys only what she knows. Background can be asked about; do not deliver it as a biography at the doorway.
- The mother has questions and boundaries. Admission of cruelty does not compel her to forgive.
- Narration describes an action or an observable detail. Keep medical/design explanations in documentation.
- Future events must not appear in day names or continuation labels. A neutral date is enough.
- A remembered event must have occurred on that player's route. Conditional belongings are conditional options, not code logic in a label.

## Play

The stair opening offers control within 40 words. Existing reception flags retain their consequences. A button-sorting interaction makes the relationship reciprocal: Hoda can help, get something wrong, retry or be dismissed. War news interrupts ordinary work; the player chooses how to listen and share it. The paper comparison makes checking a lead an action. The visible mother's reply changes the search into communication.

During illness the player chooses company, activity and words, not medical survival. Every route receives care. Hoda appears as a patient; adults appear during the final meeting. Following her death, collecting her belongings is a player action. The final meeting, messenger and refusal routes each finish with a physical action. Requests for keepsakes do not override the mother's consent.
