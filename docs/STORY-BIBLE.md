# Story bible — بيتنا فين؟

Reference for writers and code. Approved 26 October 2026 (shared-life revision). Where an older document disagrees, this one wins.
The story is fictional family history set around documented events (see STORY-HISTORY-1973.md).
The Suez-siege / 1974 alternative is **not** adopted.

## Why it takes until 18 November — the search, step by step

Each step is the obvious next move for someone in that position, and each one produces a result.

1. **4 Oct (ch1).** Sonia's room has new tenants; Naima leaves her name with them so any letter for Sonia is handed to her. Sonia's things are with the landlord. *Result:* a contact point for letters.
2. **7 Oct (war chapter).** Naima's son goes to عم فوزي: the mother left in spring for a clothing factory in Alexandria; he doesn't have an address. Fatma and Hoda write a letter "to the factory" with her name; the son posts it. *Result:* the city and the employer.
3. **22 Oct.** The landlord hands over Sonia's tin box: the mother's old letters with the **hostel address**, and one letter that arrived after Sonia left. It was written at the start of October: the factory works two shifts and leave is stopped; she has found a room with two co-workers and will come for Hoda "after pay day". Hoda reads it herself. They write back to the hostel. *Result:* a real address, and the reason she hasn't come.
4. **3 Nov (ch2).** The letter "to the factory" came back undelivered; nothing yet from the hostel. Naima's son asks again at the shop. *Result:* none — the failed generic address explains why step 3 mattered.
5. **10 Nov (ch3).** The hostel supervisor replies: the woman with that name there "has a six-year-old" — a different woman. She adds that "another one" moved out last month. Fatma compares age, the shop, Suez: the mother is the one who moved out but **still works at the factory**. Fatma writes back asking the supervisor to hand a letter to her **on the factory floor**, and Hoda adds a detail only her mother would know (chosen by the player). *Result:* the address correction.
6. **18 Nov (ch4).** The mother's reply, through a co-worker: she answers Hoda's detail, gives her new room's address, and will come on her leave at the start of December. She sends money for Hoda's needs (a small amount). *Result:* contact.
7. **22–26 Nov.** Fatma writes about the measles; the mother replies she is trying to bring her leave forward. *Result:* she knows Hoda is ill.
8. **30 Nov.** Severe breathing trouble; hospital. Naima's son sends a **telegram** to the new address. The mother takes the first morning train and arrives on **1 Dec**. Hoda died in the night. Naima tells her before anyone else does.

What each person knows is tracked by flags (`search_*`, `lead_*`, `proof_detail`, `mother_*`). Lines may only mention a step the player's route has reached.

## Illness and death

Measles (rash, fever, cough, isolation from other children), then severe pneumonia — a recognised complication, not the typical outcome. Care is unconditional: food choices, money, gifts and relationship values never change the illness or the outcome. There is no rescue choice. During the illness the player chooses company and small activities, not survival. No treatment instructions are given.

## Shared habits (the core of play)

- **Sock ball**: stairs (first meeting) → counting game → the "crossing" game over the tabliya (her rule) → off the wall, counting in twos (school) → from her bed, rolled on the blanket → alone, against the wall, nobody counting.
- **Button tin**: work and company at once. She learns sizes; she catches Fatma's mistakes; the red button.
- **Clothes**: what she wears to school, the tear, the patch, the sleeve, the cardigan, the red ribbon. Visible in every later scene.
- **Counting**: hers, then Fatma's.

## Money

Only one price is sourced (bread at half a piaster until 1977). All other prices are game approximations, not historical claims (see RESEARCH-FLAGS #9). Income comes from named sewing orders; extras (eggs, ta'meya, a notebook, thread) come out of the coin tin, which is visible on the machine. Free alternatives exist and are often the warmer choice: borrowing thread from Naima, a scrap-satin patch, playing.

## Endings

The meeting (now / in two days), sending the things with Naima, or keeping them for now. Every ending closes on a physical action drawn from what this playthrough actually did (the ball, the counting, the red button, the patched dress, the second chair). The mother is told of the death before any choice; she can refuse a keepsake.
