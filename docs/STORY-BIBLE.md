## Why the mother arrives on 1 December — the chain, step by step

Every step is the obvious next move for that person. Every delay comes from a fact established early in the game, not from a coincidence.

1. **28 Sep – 3 Oct.** Sonia collapses and is operated on. Naima keeps Hoda for a week, but her flat is full. Sonia's room is re-let on 3 Oct, and her things go into the landlord's storeroom.
2. **4 Oct (ch1).** Sonia can talk for the first time. She tells Naima the mother is in Libya and coming on 1 Dec, and that the address is on the letters in her tin box. Naima brings Hoda to Fatma that afternoon.
3. **5 Oct (ch1 morning).** Naima's son fetches the tin box from the landlord. The mother's September letter gives the contractor's office in Benghazi, says mail is collected on the first of the month, and repeats "أول ديسمبر". Fatma and Hoda write at once; Hoda adds a detail of her own. A telegram is considered and dropped: it would wait at the same office until payday, and it costs per word.
4. **6–25 Oct (war chapter).** The war slows the post. On 22 Oct (the ceasefire), the tenants of Sonia's old room hand Naima a letter that arrived there late: the mother's letter of 3 Oct, written before the war. She is worried that Sonia hasn't replied, and tells Hoda to tear one calendar page a day until December. Fatma and Hoda write a second letter to the office. *Result:* Hoda's calendar ritual, and the mother's voice.
5. **1 Nov.** Payday: the mother receives both letters together, the first news in a month. She replies at once.
6. **3 Nov (ch2).** Naima: "it's the third; she has it now". Sonia is discharged to Fayoum.
7. **10 Nov (ch3).** The mother's reply arrives. It answers Hoda's detail, gives the workshop's own address (faster than the office), thanks Fatma, encloses a 50-piaster money order for Hoda (the player decides: Hoda's own envelope, or the household tin), and confirms 1 Dec. She can't leave before the contract ends without losing the year's ticket and the wages she went there for.
8. **11 Nov.** Hoda and Fatma reply to the workshop.
9. **18–22 Nov (ch4).** A cough on the 18th; the doctor on the 19th; measles diagnosed on the 22nd (there is measles among the school children). Fatma writes to the workshop: measles, the doctor is following it, don't worry. Measles was common, and nobody expects it to turn dangerous.
10. **28 Nov.** A telegram from the mother: "سلامة هدى. the contract ends the day after tomorrow; coming 1 Dec". 29 Nov: Hoda can't get up; Fatma tears the page.
11. **Night of 29–30 Nov.** After a bad night, Hoda is taken to hospital at dawn with severe pneumonia after measles. Naima's son goes to the telephone exchange: an international call needs booking until the next day, so he sends a telegram to the workshop. The telegram reaches her on her last day at the workshop; the contractor puts her on the first flight. Hoda dies that night despite treatment.
12. **1 Dec (ch5).** They wait for her until noon, not knowing when she will land; the burial cannot wait longer. The mother arrives as people come back from the cemetery; Naima goes to her and tells her. Fatma goes home. **2 Dec:** the mother, who slept at Naima's, asks to meet Fatma; afterwards she goes to Sonia in Fayoum.

There are no returned letters, no wrong women and no missing people. The waiting itself is the story: the date was fixed from the start, and the illness was faster than everyone.

## Illness and death

Measles (rash, fever, cough, isolation from other children), then severe pneumonia — a recognised complication, not the typical outcome. Care is unconditional: food choices, money, gifts and relationship values never change the illness or the outcome. There is no rescue choice. During the illness the player chooses company and small activities, not survival. No treatment instructions are given.

## Shared habits (the core of play)

- **Sock ball**: stairs (first meeting) → counting game → the "crossing" game over the tabliya (her rule) → off the wall, counting in twos (school) → from her bed, rolled on the blanket → alone, against the wall, nobody counting.
- **Button tin**: work and company at once. She learns sizes; she catches Fatma's mistakes; the red button.
- **Clothes**: what she wears to school, the tear, the patch, the sleeve, the cardigan, the red ribbon. Visible in every later scene.
- **Counting**: hers, then Fatma's. The calendar turns the waiting into a number every morning.
- **Braiding** (`CombGame`): each tap is one stroke; hurrying pulls her hair; slow strokes and she talks. The red ribbon is refused except on the day of the mother's letter.
- **Rehearsing the arrival** (ch4): Hoda plays her mother at the door; in ch5 Fatma remembers where Hoda told her to stand.

## Money

Only one price is sourced (bread at half a piaster until 1977). All other prices are game approximations, not historical claims (see RESEARCH-FLAGS #9). Income comes from named sewing orders; extras (eggs, ta'meya, a notebook, thread) come out of the coin tin, which is visible on the machine. Free alternatives exist and are often the warmer choice: borrowing thread from Naima, a scrap-satin patch, playing.

## Endings

The meeting (now / in two days), sending the things with Naima, or keeping them for now. Every ending closes on a physical action drawn from what this playthrough actually did (the ball, the counting, the red button, the patched dress, the second chair). The mother is told of the death before any choice; she can refuse a keepsake.
