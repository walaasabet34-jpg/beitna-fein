# Story and historical continuity — October–December 1973

This is a fictional family story around documented historical events. Character biographies, dialogue, school attendance dates, hospital course and contact-chain events are fictional. They are not testimony or an account of a real child.

## Documented anchors

- Egypt's crossing of the Suez Canal began on 6 October 1973. The family hears the news during the afternoon and celebrates. Their dialogue is invented; no precise bulletin time or recording is claimed.
  - Egyptian State Information Service: https://sis.gov.eg/en/egypt/society/days-and-occasions/october-1973-victory/
  - Contemporary US discussion documenting the crossing on 6 October: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v25/d106
- Israeli forces crossed to the west bank by 16 October. The chapter uses retrospective narration and limited household knowledge; the early crossing celebration is not rewritten as false.
  - https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v25/d191 (contemporary 16 October meeting, reporting Israeli forces across the canal).
- Resolution 338 called for a ceasefire on 22 October; resolution 339 reaffirmed it on 23 October. Fighting continued. Resolution 340 of 25 October called again for a ceasefire and established UNEF II. There is no claim that all fighting stopped or families returned immediately on 22 October.
  - https://peacekeeping.un.org/sites/default/files/past/unef2backgr2.html
  - https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-207378/
- Measles was widely contracted in childhood before widespread vaccination. Pneumonia is a recognized potentially fatal complication. A nine-year-old's fatal case is medically plausible; it is not the typical outcome, and no specific Cairo outbreak, vaccination history or 1973 hospital protocol is asserted.
  - https://www.emro.who.int/health-topics/measles/vaccine-vaccination.html
  - https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/measles
  - https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/vaccine-preventable-diseases-surveillance-standards-measles

## Narrative rules implemented

- The mother is sought through a continuous chain of relatives/acquaintances established before the illness. Her identity is checked against family details and a known relative. No invented government office or guaranteed official name list.
- The mother learns of Hoda's death on every final route before the player chooses whether to meet her. Refusing the meeting does not hide her child's fate.
- Medical care is unconditional and not purchased with relationship points or money. Death is fixed in this story; bedside choices do not offer a false rescue mechanic.
- The relationship before illness is remembered separately. A few gentle bedside choices do not erase earlier harshness.
- Acknowledging cruelty does not earn automatic forgiveness. Warm play preserves specific memories rather than preventing death.
- A requested keepsake is not taken without consent. The mother's refusal is respected. Withholding belongings is left as an unresolved harm, not a completed reconciliation.
- The crossing-day drawing and shared joy can recur in the grief chapter; the family genuinely lived those moments.
- Historical news is readable narrated text. No synthesized voice or unverified recording is presented as a genuine 1973 bulletin. Existing radio date/rights gates remain active.
